Engineering Design and Communication Project | B3 Coffee

Background

For my Engineering Design and Communication class, my teammates and I were assigned a semester-long design project for a real company. We worked with B3 Coffee in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, a cafe dedicated to providing vocational opportunities for people of all abilities.

The Problem

All employees (regardless of disability status) have trouble seeing the lines in the pre-existing metal pitchers, which then affects the amount of milk they use and the quality of the drinks they serve.

Design Process

Brainstorming

My teammates and I first started by researching existing solutions, conducting different brainstorming techniques, and interviewing employees at B3 Coffee to get as much background information as possible.

Design Criteria

After interviewing B3 employees, we wrote design criteria to guide our design process. The solution had to be simple, intuitive, durable, accurate, and replicable. We then made Pugh Scoring Charts and other quantitative metrics to grade our prototypes and ideas.

Prototyping

We went through many stages of the prototyping process. We first started with low-fidelity, incomplete prototypes, then moved to medium-fidelity, and finally, constructed two high-fidelity prototypes to thoroughly test and evaluate.

Final Product

Our final product was the Honeycomb Clip. The clip attaches to the side of the metal pitcher and the honeycomb grid indicates where the employee stops pouring milk. We created three different clips for three different milk volumes that are used at the cafe.

The clip is made of laser-cut acrylic, with the design made on Adobe Illustrator. Along with multiple of each clip, we made a stand for organization.

Land Surveying & AutoCAD Mapping

Background

In my Engineering the Planet Class (Civil Engineering class), our final project was to survey a building on Duke’s campus and construct a horizontal and vertical view of the building in AutoCAD.

Surveying

My partner and I surveyed the Physics Building on our campus. To ensure accuracy, measurements were tied into permanent benchmarks on campus. For every point we took, we referenced it back to our benchmarks so we always had a point in reference to two other points in space. Throughout the surveying, we used an auto level, optical theodolite, and electronic theodolite.

Features surveyed included walkways, trees, bushes, benches, windows, and doors. Using the optical theodolite, we were able to calculate the vertical angles for the building facade and vertical profile. Every measurement taken was written down in our field notebook using proper field techniques.

AutoCAD

I was in charge of creating the horizontal view from the data we collected. Through the use of different layers, I input points after each day we surveyed, so every day was on a different layer. After all of the days, I lined up the benchmarks. Included in the horizontal view is a title block, legend, and compass.

Room Filtration System (RFS)

Background

My team and I were tasked with designing a Room Filtration System (RFS) that bring the particulate matter of a room to a safe level of 10 ug/m3, along with a Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) of 300 m3/hr.

Design Process

My teammates and I brainstormed different air filter prototypes. We had iterations with wheels, 3 filters, 1 filter, and 4 filters. After conducting further testing, we decided to pursue a system with four MERV 11 filters and a box fan on top.

Testing and Data Analysis

In order to test the RFS, we placed it in a sealed room with a candle lit, and measured the amount of particulate matter in the room over time.

Our team then calculated the total loss rate and air exchange rate. We observed a difference of +8.82 hr-1 between the control and the experimental tests. This highlights how the air filter was effective and filtered more air in less time. This is because we utilized four air filters total, which allowed for more air filtration overall.

The CADR of the air filter using the dimensions of the room provided (75 m3) is 661.5 m3/hr. This is significantly higher than the required 300 m3/hr goal, and would therefore save everyone from the zombie apocalypse.
